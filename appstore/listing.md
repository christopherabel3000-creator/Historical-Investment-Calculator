## Description (4,000)

What if you'd invested? Pick an amount, a start date and how often you'd invest, and see what it would be worth today in five low-cost funds: an S&P 500 index fund, a Treasury bond fund, a gold fund, a bitcoin fund and a money market fund. Or build your own mix.

REAL HISTORY, REAL FUNDS
Returns go back as far as 1871 for stocks and 1928 for bonds and Treasury bills. Each fund is modeled on a real low-cost fund, with its fee taken out, so the numbers are what an ordinary investor could actually have earned.

ONE TIME, MONTHLY OR YEARLY
Invest a lump sum, or a set amount every month or year, like a 401(k) or IRA. See the total you put in, what it grew to, and the yearly return.

TODAY'S DOLLARS
Switch on inflation adjustment to see every result in today's buying power.

YOUR OWN MIX
Split your money across the five funds, like 60% stocks and 40% bonds, with optional yearly rebalancing.

BEST AND WORST YEARS
See each fund's best and worst year in your time range, and how much your own account gained or lost in each.

WILL MY MONEY LAST?
Plan your retirement withdrawals. Enter a balance and how much you'll take out, and see how often the money would have run out, replayed across every real stretch of history and 10,000 simulated futures. Or work backward: enter the monthly income you want, and see how much you'd need to save for it to last.

UPDATED EVERY WEEKDAY
Prices are updated after each market close. The app works offline with the latest prices it has.

MADE FOR VOICEOVER
Built by a developer who uses a screen reader. Every result is written out as a sentence, every chart has a matching table, and every control is labeled.

SIMPLE AND PRIVATE
No ads. Nothing to buy. No accounts. No tracking. What you type never leaves your device.

This is a historical illustration for learning, not investment advice. Past returns don't predict future ones.

## Notes for the reviewer

Apple asks every new app for six things in this field: a screen recording made on a real iPhone, and answers 2 to 6 below. Only the recording link changes.

What If I'd Invested is an educational calculator that shows how past investments would have grown, built to be fully usable with VoiceOver by a developer who uses a screen reader. The build was tested on a physical iPhone through TestFlight before submission.

1. SCREEN RECORDING
Recorded on a physical iPhone running the latest iOS, starting from launching the app: RECORDING LINK
The voice heard in the recording is VoiceOver, since the developer uses a screen reader.
The app has no account registration or login, no user-generated content, and no paid content or purchases. So those flows don't exist to show.

2. PURPOSE AND AUDIENCE
A historical investment returns calculator. Users enter an amount, a start date and how often they'd invest, and see what it would be worth today in five low-cost index-style funds (S&P 500, Treasury bonds, gold, bitcoin and a money market fund) or a mix, with fees and inflation included. A second part tests a retirement withdrawal plan against real market history. It is for people learning about investing and planning for retirement, and especially for blind and low-vision users: every result is written out as a sentence and every chart has a matching table.

3. HOW TO USE IT
No account, login or sample files are needed. The calculator opens with an example already filled in ($10,000 invested in January 1990) and updates as you change any field. Scroll down to "Will my money last?" and choose "How much I need to save" to see how much savings a monthly income would need.

4. EXTERNAL SERVICES
Only one: when the app opens, it downloads the latest price file from the calculator's own website on GitHub Pages, so prices stay current. Nothing about the user is sent. There are no authentication services, payment processors, ad networks, analytics or AI services. Every calculation runs on the device, and the app works offline with the last prices it downloaded.

5. REGIONS
The app works the same in every region. It is in English only and uses US market data in US dollars. There is no region-specific content or behavior.

6. REGULATED INDUSTRIES AND THIRD-PARTY MATERIAL
The app does not provide financial services. It does not hold, move or invest money, connect to any bank or brokerage, recommend specific investments, or collect any financial information from users. It is an educational calculator based on published historical data, and it says on screen that it is not investment advice. The historical data comes from public academic and government sources (Robert Shiller, Aswath Damodaran, FRED and the Bureau of Labor Statistics) and from Financial Modeling Prep under a data display agreement. The fonts are used under the SIL Open Font License.
