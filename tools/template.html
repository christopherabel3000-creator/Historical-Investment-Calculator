About the numbers

  • S&P 500 figures show a low-cost index fund charging 0.04% a year, the fee on Vanguard's 500 Index Admiral Shares (VFIAX), with dividends reinvested. That fee is applied to the whole history, including years before index funds existed.
  • Through , S&P 500 returns come from Robert Shiller's monthly dataset going back to 1871. After that, they come from the SPY ETF's dividend-adjusted prices, with SPY's own fee added back.
  • Treasury bill figures show a money market fund charging 0.11% a year, the fee on Vanguard's Federal Money Market Fund (VMFXX). Returns come from Aswath Damodaran's annual 3-month T-bill returns from 1928 on, spread evenly across each year's months, and 2026 uses an estimate of 3.9% a year.
  • Treasury bond figures show a bond fund charging 0.03% a year, the fee on Vanguard's Intermediate-Term Treasury ETF (VGIT), holding 10-year Treasury bonds with interest reinvested. Returns come from Aswath Damodaran's annual 10-year Treasury bond returns from 1928 through 2025, spread evenly across each year's months. 2026 is calculated from month-end 10-year yields. VGIT itself holds somewhat shorter bonds, so its ups and downs are a bit smaller.
  • Gold figures show a gold fund charging 0.10% a year, the fee on SPDR Gold MiniShares (GLDM), using monthly gold prices. They start in January 1975, the first month Americans could legally own gold again after a ban that began in 1933. Before 1971 the government also fixed gold's price.
  • For monthly or yearly investing, each deposit buys in on the start month and every month or year after it, through . "Per year" is the money-weighted annual return, which accounts for when each deposit went in.
  • Results include the fund fees above but ignore taxes and inflation.
  • This is a historical illustration, not investment advice. Past returns don't predict future ones.
